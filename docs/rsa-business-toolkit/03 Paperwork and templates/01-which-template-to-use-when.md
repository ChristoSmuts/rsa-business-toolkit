## Invoice numbering

Number invoices in order with no gaps: INV-0001, INV-0002, INV-0003.

A gap in the sequence looks like a hidden sale to SARS. If you cancel an invoice, do not delete it. Issue a credit note against it and keep both.

Use the same idea for quotes: QUO-0001.

## If you are a Pty Ltd: the header line that is required by law

Every quote, invoice, receipt, delivery note and letter must carry the company's full registered name and registration number, under section 32 of the Companies Act. If you use a trading name, show both:

```
MOKOENA MOTORS
A trading name of Mokoena Holdings (Pty) Ltd | Reg. No. 2026/123456/07
```

Three rules that save you money:

Put a validity date on every quote. Material prices move.

Write down what is not included. Most disputes are about work the customer assumed was in the price.

Get a deposit before you buy materials. A signed quote plus a deposit is a contract.

## Template 3: invoice if you ARE registered for VAT

Only use this once SARS has issued your VAT number.

There are three levels, based on the total including VAT.

A tax invoice is not required where the amount is R50 or less, although you still need supporting documentation for an input tax deduction.

For supplies from R50 up to R5,000, an abridged tax invoice is allowed. It needs most of the same information, but you do not have to include the recipient's details.

For a supply of more than R5,000, a full tax invoice must contain: the words "Tax Invoice", "VAT Invoice" or "Invoice"; the supplier's name, address and VAT registration number; the recipient's name, address and, where they are registered, their VAT registration number; a serial number and the date of issue; and a description of the goods or services supplied and the quantity or volume. It must also show the value of the supply, the VAT charged, and the total.

> **In plain words:** For any sale over R5,000, your invoice must show: the words "Tax Invoice", your name, address and VAT number, the customer's name and address (and their VAT number if they have one), an invoice number and date, what you sold and how much of it, the price before VAT, the VAT, and the total.

You must issue the document within 21 days of completing the sale or delivering the service.

All the required criteria must be met. SARS checks invoices against these criteria, and if a single one is missing the entire invoice is disallowed.

```
[YOUR BUSINESS NAME]
[Physical address]
VAT Registration Number: 4XXXXXXXXX
[Phone] | [Email]

TAX INVOICE

Invoice number: INV-0001
Date: [DD Month YYYY]

TO
[Customer name]
[Customer physical address]
[Customer VAT number, if they are a vendor]

-------------------------------------------------------------
Description Qty Unit price Amount
-------------------------------------------------------------
[Goods or services] 1 R 0.00 R 0.00
-------------------------------------------------------------
 Subtotal (excl. VAT) R 0.00
 VAT @ 15% R 0.00
 TOTAL (incl. VAT) R 0.00
-------------------------------------------------------------

[Banking details]
```

Working with 15%: to add VAT, multiply by 1.15. To find the VAT inside a VAT-inclusive price, multiply by 15/115.

If you sell second-hand goods, the description must say so.

## Template 6: cash book

One spreadsheet. Seven columns. This is your whole accounting system in year one.

| Date | Description | Customer or supplier | Money in | Money out | Category | Slip? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Categories to use: Sales, Stock, Materials, Fuel, Data and airtime, Tools and equipment, Rent, Bank charges, Advertising, Insurance, Other.

Rules:

Enter every transaction. Every one, including the R38 you spent on cable ties.

Take a photo of every slip the day you get it. Put it in a folder named by month. Tick the "Slip?" column.

Reconcile against your bank statement once a month. If the spreadsheet total and the bank balance disagree, find out why that same day.

If you use turnover tax, your records are lighter. You only track amounts received, dividends declared, and assets or liabilities over R10,000.

## Prompts to generate the rest

### Terms and conditions

```
I am a [BUSINESS TYPE] in South Africa. I sell [WHAT] to [WHO].
I am [a sole proprietor / a registered company].
[I sell online / I sell in person / both.]

Write terms and conditions for my business in simple English that a
Grade 8 learner can read.

Cover:
- What I will do and what the customer must do
- Quotes, deposits, and payment terms
- What happens if a customer cancels
- What happens if I have to cancel
- Delivery or completion timing
- Returns, refunds, and faults
- Limits on what I am responsible for
- How disputes get resolved

Make sure it complies with the Consumer Protection Act 68 of 2008.
Where the Act gives the customer a right I cannot remove, say so in the
document rather than trying to remove it.

[If selling online, add:] I sell online, so also comply with sections 43,
44, and 46 of the Electronic Communications and Transactions Act 25 of
2002, including the seven-day cooling-off right.

After the document, list every clause where you are unsure and where I
should check with a lawyer.
```

The Consumer Protection Act requires plain language. A document your customer cannot understand can be unenforceable, so do not ask the AI to make it sound legal.

### A simple service agreement

```
I am a [BUSINESS TYPE] in South Africa. I need a short written agreement
for jobs over R[AMOUNT].

Write a one-page agreement in simple English covering:
- Who the parties are
- Exactly what work I will do
- Exactly what is not included
- Price, deposit, and payment dates
- Start date and expected finish date
- What happens if the customer changes the scope
- What happens if the customer does not pay
- Who owns the work when it is done
- How either side can cancel
- Which South African law applies

Use [SQUARE BRACKETS] for anything I fill in per job.
Keep it to one page. Do not use legal words where a plain word works.
```

## Documents you probably do not need yet

A business plan. Only needed to raise money from a bank or investor.

An employment contract. Only when you hire someone. When you do, get a proper one, because the Basic Conditions of Employment Act is strict and mistakes are expensive.

A non-disclosure agreement. Rarely needed by a small supplier. Most clients who need one will send you theirs.

A shareholders agreement. Only if you have more than one shareholder. If you ever take a partner, get this before you take their money, not after.

A company stamp. It has no legal meaning in South Africa.
