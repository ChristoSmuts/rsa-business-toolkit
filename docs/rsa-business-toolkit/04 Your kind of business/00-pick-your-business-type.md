## How much regulation each type carries

This is a rough guide to how much setup work to expect before you can legally trade.

| Business type | Setup effort | Main reason |
|---|---|---|
| Professional and creative | Lowest | Usually no licence at all |
| Services and trades | Low to medium | Some trades need professional registration |
| Retail and online | Medium | Consumer law, and product rules for some goods |
| Beauty and personal care | Medium to high | Health inspection and municipal licence |
| Food | High | Certificate of Acceptability, training, inspections |
| Vehicle dealing | High | Three separate registration layers |

If you are choosing between business ideas and money is tight, the left column of this table is worth reading as a cost comparison. A food business can take two to six months before it may legally open. A consulting business can invoice tomorrow.

## What everyone needs, regardless of type

From `01-core/`:

- A separate bank account for business money
- SARS registration and filing, even at zero income
- POPIA information officer registration, free, at inforegulator.org.za
- Honest advertising and Consumer Protection Act compliance
- Records kept for five years

## What nobody needs on day one

- A registered company, unless a customer demands it
- VAT registration, unless turnover passes R2.3 million or the maths favours it
- A paid B-BBEE certificate, since the affidavit is free under R10 million turnover
- A business plan, unless you are asking someone for money
- A website, before you have customers
