# Start here

This toolkit is for one person running a business in South Africa. You are the owner. You are also the only worker. You have little money and little time.

Everything here is free or very cheap. Every step tells you if it is required by law, or if it is only a good idea.

## The honest truth first

You do not need most of the things people tell you to get.

You can legally start selling today, under your own name, with no company registration, no logo, no website, and no accountant. Many people spend R15,000 and three months on setup before they earn one rand. Do not do that.

Earn money first. Add the formal parts as the business grows.

## Order to do things

Work through these in order. Do not skip ahead.

1. Sell something and get paid. Even once. This proves people want it.
2. Open a separate bank account for business money. Keep business money and home money apart from day one. Ask the bank what your instant payment limits are; you will need them.

 If you will work from home with no shop, read [Working from home and meeting safely](09-working-from-home-and-safety.md) before your first sale. It is short and it covers the habits that stop you being scammed.
3. Pick a name and a simple look. Use file [Branding prompts](../02%20Branding%20and%20marketing/01-branding-prompts.md).
4. Make your quote and invoice templates. Use file [Which template to use when](../03%20Paperwork%20and%20templates/01-which-template-to-use-when.md).
5. Check if your kind of business needs a licence to sell. Read [What you need to sell things](04-what-you-need-to-sell-things.md), then open your business type in `04-business-types/`. Some businesses may not trade without a licence.
6. Sort out SARS. Use file [Tax and SARS](03-tax-and-sars.md). Do this even if you earn nothing.
7. Decide if you need a registered company. Use file [Register: what you actually need](02-register.md). Most solo businesses do not need one in year one. If you decide yes, read [Running a Pty Ltd](06-running-a-pty-ltd.md) before you register, not after.

Steps 5 and 6 are the two that get people fined. Do not leave them for later.

## How to use the AI prompts

The prompts are written for any AI chat tool. They work in Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, or Copilot. The free versions are fine.

Rules for using them:

Copy one prompt at a time. Paste it. Wait for the answer. Do not paste the next prompt until you are happy with the current one.

Every branding prompt gives you 5 options. Read all 5. Pick the one closest to what you want, even if it is not perfect. Then tell the AI what you like and what you do not like, and ask for 5 new versions. Do this two or three times. The third round is usually much better than the first.

Never accept the first answer. The first answer is the average answer.

Start a new chat for each big task. A chat that is too long makes the AI forget things.

The AI will sometimes be wrong about South African law, prices, and forms. It is a writing helper, not a lawyer. Check anything legal against the official website named in this toolkit.

## About the numbers in this toolkit

Tax rules, fees, and thresholds were checked in September 2026. The tax year running now is 1 March 2026 to 28 February 2027.

South African tax thresholds changed a lot in the 2026 Budget. Older blogs and older AI answers still use the old numbers. If a website tells you the VAT threshold is R1 million, that site is out of date.

Before you pay anything or file anything, check the current figure on the official site:

- SARS: www.sars.gov.za
- Company registration: www.bizportal.gov.za
- Data protection: inforegulator.org.za
- Vehicles: www.natis.gov.za

This toolkit is general information. It is not legal advice or tax advice. For anything large or unusual, pay a registered tax practitioner for one hour of their time. One hour is cheaper than one penalty.
