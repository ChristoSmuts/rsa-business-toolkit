# Sources and verification register

Everything in this toolkit that states a fee, a threshold, a form number, or a legal requirement is listed here with the source it came from.

All sources were checked on 13 September 2026.

## How to use this file

This file is for checking, not for reading from top to bottom. Come here when you want to know where a fact came from, or to confirm a number before you act on it.

How the entries are laid out:

- **[Official]** means a government department or regulator wrote it. Trust these first.
- "Supports:" lists the facts in the toolkit that come from that source.
- "Secondary" means a law firm, accountant, or business website. Useful for explanation, but confirm against the official source.
- Act names like "Companies Act 71 of 2008" mean the law itself. Number 71 was passed in 2008. You can read any Act free at www.gov.za.
- "s32" means section 32 of that Act.

If a name or a word here is unfamiliar, it is explained in [Glossary](01-glossary.md) in this folder.

---

Use this file to verify anything before you act on it. Where a claim matters financially or legally, open the official link and confirm the number yourself. Fees and thresholds change, usually in February and April.

Official sources are marked **[Official]**. These are government or regulator publications. Everything else is secondary and should be treated as a pointer to the official source, not as authority.

## Paying yourself from a company

**[Official] SARS — Comprehensive Guide to Dividends Tax**
https://www.sars.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/Ops/Guides/LAPD-IT-G19-Comprehensive-Guide-to-Dividends-Tax.pdf
Supports: the section 64E(4) deemed dividend provisions, including the nature and amount of the deemed dividend and its deemed date of payment. Read this before acting on any debit loan account.

**[Official] SARS — Budget 2026 FAQ** (as above)
Supports: the official interest rate for the fringe benefit on interest-free or low-interest loans, 7.75% per annum from 1 December 2025.

Underlying law: Income Tax Act 58 of 1962 — s64E(4) (deemed dividends on low-interest loans to shareholders), the Seventh Schedule (fringe benefits), paragraph 11C of the Fourth Schedule (deemed remuneration for private company directors). Companies Act 71 of 2008 — s4 and s46 (distributions and the solvency and liquidity test).

Case law: Cohen NO v Segal, Witwatersrand Local Division, judgment 28 April 1970 — a dividend may generally only be declared out of profits, not capital. Confirm the law report citation with an attorney before relying on it in correspondence.

Secondary:
- Polity / ENSafrica, Directors of private companies and PAYE — https://www.polity.org.za/article/directors-of-private-companies-are-no-longer-subject-to-paye---true-or-false-2018-09-25 — confirms paragraph 11C was repealed with effect from 1 March 2017, and that section 7B governs the timing of variable remuneration.
- SimplePay, Deemed Remuneration — https://www.simplepay.co.za/help/payroll-concepts/deemed-remuneration/ — confirms deemed remuneration should no longer be used from 1 March 2017 and that directors are taxed on actual remuneration.
- **Not used:** TaxTim's article on a director's PAYE and UIF deductions, and PATC's equivalent, both still describe the repealed deemed remuneration formula as current. They were checked and rejected. This is a good example of why the official position matters more than a well-ranked article.
- The Tax Faculty, Dividends interest on loan — https://taxfaculty.ac.za/news/read/dividends-interest-on-loan — that the capital of a debit loan is not itself a deemed dividend, only the interest shortfall.
- MMS Cloud Accounting — https://www.mmscloudaccounting.co.za/on-the-right-side-of-directors-loan-accounts/ — the three ways to clear a debit loan account before year end.
- The Beancounter — https://thebeancounter.co.za/dear-director-hows-your-loan-account-looking/ — practical timing, and why waiting for the financial statements is too late.
- Govchain — https://www.govchain.co.za/blog/the-best-way-to-pay-yourself-as-a-company-owner — the risk when a loan is written off.

**Two conflicts recorded, both flagged in the toolkit text:**

1. *Scope of the deemed dividend.* Some sources state the deemed dividend is 20% of the entire debit loan balance. The Tax Faculty and the wording of s64E(4) support the narrower position that only the foregone interest is deemed a dividend. The toolkit takes the narrower position and says so.

2. *UIF for a sole director.* ClearComply and eqeight indicate UIF applies to a director paid through payroll even as the only employee. Other sources indicate UIF and SDL do not apply where the director receives director's fees and is not an employee under an employment contract. The toolkit presents both and advises getting advice rather than picking one.

## You are the business: UIF, COIDA, courts and continuity

**UIF and the self-employed.** Unemployment Insurance Contributions Act 2002, section 1 — "employee" means a natural person receiving remuneration for services rendered, excluding an independent contractor. The Tax Faculty applies this directly: https://taxfaculty.ac.za/faq/general_faqs/solution/1170 — a sole proprietor cannot contribute to the UIF because a sole proprietor cannot be an employee who becomes unemployed.

**COIDA and working directors.** The Act's definition of employee includes a director or member of a body corporate who has entered into a contract of service, in so far as they act within the scope of that employment, and excludes shareholders or silent partners who only receive dividends or profit share. See https://www.eohcb.co.za/post/coida-who-must-register-for-coida and https://www.mdacc.co.za/do-i-need-to-register-for-workmens-compensation/. That a working director paid a monthly salary must be registered and submit a Return of Earnings even as the only employee is from ClearComply's 2026 guide: https://www.clearcomply.co.za/blog/coida-compensation-fund-south-africa. Sole proprietors and partners are not employees under COIDA: https://labourguide.co.za/coid/compensation-fund-registrations/compensation-fund-registration

**Small Claims Court, who may sue.** Small Claims Courts Act 61 of 1984, section 7(1) — only a natural person may institute an action, and a juristic person may become a party only as defendant. Consolidated text: https://www.saflii.org/za/legis/consol_act/scca1984214/. Confirmed by Legal Aid South Africa (https://legal-aid.co.za/2018/09/26/instituting-action-in-the-small-claims-court-guide/), Werksmans Attorneys (https://werksmans.com/a-brief-overview-of-the-small-claims-court-2026-update/) and the Department of Justice (https://www.justice.gov.za/scc/scc_info.htm). A nominated director or officer may appear for a juristic person that is a defendant. The three-year prescription period is from the Werksmans overview.

**Death of a sole director and shareholder.** Companies Act 71 of 2008 — a person ceases to be a director on death; directors are appointed by shareholders. Administration of Estates Act 66 of 1965, section 13(1) — a deceased estate must be reported to the Master of the High Court within 14 days. South African commentary on the resulting paralysis, the dependence on the MOI, and the Saga Wines matter: Al Baraka Bank legal services (https://www.albaraka.co.za/blogs/legalease-3-2023/business-owners-beware-your-death-could-result-in-the-closure-of-your-business), Shandu Attorneys (https://shanduattorneys.co.za/2019/11/27/business-continuity-risk-death-of-a-companys-sole-shareholder-and-sole-director/), FindAnAttorney (https://www.findanattorney.co.za/content_company-law-deceased-estate) and Iota (https://www.iota.co.za/news-1/business-continuity-of-sole-shareholder-and-director-dying), which gives the two-weeks-to-three-months estimate for appointing an executor.

Caution: several widely-read articles on this topic are written for the UK Model Articles or Australia's section 201F. **Neither applies in South Africa.** The position here depends on your own MOI. Ask an attorney.

## Colour research and signage materials

**Colour psychology.** The toolkit deliberately avoids colour-meaning charts. The position is based on:

- Elliot, A. J. and Maier, M. A., "Color Psychology: Effects of Perceiving Color on Psychological Functioning in Humans", *Annual Review of Psychology* (2014) — no universal meaning for any colour; effects are context-dependent and vary with saturation, brightness, product category and culture.
- Labrecque, L. I. and Milne, G. R., "Exciting Red and Competent Blue: The Importance of Color in Marketing", *Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science* (2012) — consistency between brand colour and perceived brand personality predicts preference better than the colour itself.
- The frequently quoted claim that 62 to 90% of a snap product judgment comes from colour traces to a 2006 literature review by Satyendra Singh, not to a controlled buying experiment. The toolkit does not use it.

Summaries consulted: https://www.deepmarketing.it/en/blog/color-science-marketing-evidence-2026 and https://howbrandsarebuilt.com/branding-color-psychology/. The underlying papers were not read in full; if a decision turns on this, read Elliot and Maier directly.

**Signage vinyl and South African UV.**

- Sytech Supplies (South Africa) — https://www.sytech.co.za/2025/10/14/understanding-common-vinyl-failures-in-south-africas-climate-and-how-to-prevent-them/ — that South Africa sits in a Zone 3 UV climate; that vertical vinyl lasting up to ten years in Europe can fade in three years locally; that horizontal installations can lose 50 to 70% of expected lifespan. This is a supplier's blog, not an independent test. Treat the figures as an order of magnitude.
- Cast versus calendered lifespans (cast roughly 5 to 10 years outdoors, calendered roughly 1 to 5) are consistent across several independent trade sources, including https://www.craftsmenind.com/blog/fleet-graphics-lifespan and https://swiftsignquote.com/blog/cast-vs-calendered-vinyl. Local conditions and installation quality move these figures substantially.

Get your own sign maker to confirm what they are quoting before you order.
