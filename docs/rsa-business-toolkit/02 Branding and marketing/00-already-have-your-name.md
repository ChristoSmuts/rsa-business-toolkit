## Three checks still worth doing

A registered company name is not the same as owning a brand. These take twenty minutes.

**1. The domain and the handles.** Check your trading name at two or three South African domain sellers, and on Instagram, Facebook and TikTok. If they are taken, you do not have to change your name, but you need to know now, before it goes on a vehicle. You may end up trading as "Mokoena Motors" with the domain mokoenamotors.co.za taken and mokoenamotorssa.co.za free. Decide that before you print.

**2. The trade mark.** Search the CIPC trade marks database for your trading name in your class of goods or services.

A CIPC company name protects nothing in the market. It stops another company registering the same company name. It does not stop anyone trading under your brand, and it does not stop someone else trade-marking it and objecting to you.

You do not need to register a trade mark in year one. You do need to know whether someone else already holds one.

**3. Say it out loud.** Say the trading name to a native speaker of every language your customers use, and ask what it sounds like to them. Do this even if the name is already registered. It is cheaper to find out now than after the signage.

## If the name is registered but you are having second thoughts

Changing a registered company name at CIPC is possible and not expensive. But it is usually unnecessary, because the registered name and the trading name are two different things.

You can register as one thing and trade as another. "Mokoena Holdings (Pty) Ltd" can trade as "Mokoena Motors" and later add "Mokoena Solar", with no name change at CIPC.

Full detail, including when a name change is worth it, is in [Adding new lines of business](../01%20Core%20-%20applies%20to%20everyone/08-adding-new-lines.md).

## The rule that applies to you because the name is registered

Section 32 of the Companies Act requires the company's registered name and registration number on all its documents: invoices, receipts, delivery notes, letters, orders and notices, including emails and your website.

So if you market under a trading name, show both:

```
MOKOENA MOTORS
A trading name of Mokoena Holdings (Pty) Ltd | Reg. No. 2026/123456/07
```

The templates in [Which template to use when](../03%20Paperwork%20and%20templates/01-which-template-to-use-when.md) have a place for this. For a company it is not optional.

## Do you need a tagline?

Only if your name does not explain what you do.

"Mokoena Plumbing" needs no tagline. "Kumba" does. "Clipboard Motors" does, because the name is memorable but not self-explanatory.

If you need one, run Prompt 4 in the main branding file. If your name already says the trade, skip it and put the space to better use.

## Modified Prompt 2: competitor scan, visual only

The original Prompt 2 asks about naming patterns, which you no longer need. Use this version instead. It does the same job for the visual side and adds one thing the original does not: whether your existing name suits the directions you are considering.

```
Here is my brand brief:

[PASTE YOUR BRAND BRIEF]

My business name is already decided: [NAME]
I trade as: [TRADING NAME, if different]

I am not changing the name. Do not suggest alternatives.

I want to understand what branding already exists in my market in
[TOWN/CITY], South Africa, for [BUSINESS TYPE].

Based on what is typical for this kind of business in South Africa,
describe:

1. The colours most competitors in this trade use, and why
2. The logo styles they use
3. The claims they all make
4. The three most common visual clichés in this industry

Be clear that this is your general knowledge of the industry, not a
survey of my actual town. I will check real competitors myself.

Then answer two questions about my existing name:

5. What does my name already suggest visually? Does it point to a
   picture, a letter shape, or nothing in particular?
6. Is there anything about my name that would work against me visually:
   too long for a vehicle door, hard to set in type, easily confused
   with a competitor, or awkward at small sizes? Be honest.

Then give me 5 specific ways I could look deliberately different from
the rest of the trade, without looking unprofessional and without
changing the name. For each, say what the risk of that choice is.

Number the 5 ways 1 to 5. Then tell me which is weakest and why.
Then stop.
```

Question 6 is the useful one. A name you cannot change is a constraint the logo has to work around, and it is better to know about it before you choose a direction than after.

Then check reality yourself. Search your trade plus your town on Google and Facebook and look at ten competitors. Note their colours. Paste what you find into Prompt 5.
